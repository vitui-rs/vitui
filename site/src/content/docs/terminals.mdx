export const surface = (arm) => arm.split(',').slice(1).join(',').trim()

**This is the only instrument in the project that asks a real terminal rather than our model of
one.** Eight arms across seven emulator families, each driven live, each photographed through
whatever capture surface that emulator actually offers — a screen dump, a control socket, a grid
written as JSON, an AppleScript property, a protobuf over a unix socket.

Every report below is committed to the repository and regenerated by hand. **The suite reports; it
does not block**: a third party's version cannot be allowed to fail this project's pull requests, so
a disagreement arrives as a diff a human reads rather than as a red build. The gate is the replay of
the committed captures.

**Three outcomes, and keeping them apart is the point of the suite.**

- **Agreed** — the terminal did what the engine's model says it does.
- **By design** — the terminal does not do it, the project knows, and the serializer takes a
  recorded route around it. Eight such entries exist, each earned by a run and a second source
  outside the capture.
- **Cannot ask** — the *capture surface* cannot answer, which is a fact about how the terminal can
  be photographed and not about what it renders. A dump cannot tell *not stored* from *not
  serialised*, and promoting one of those to a defect would be inventing evidence.

## What the arms found

- **A terminal that reports a mode reset while the mode is set** is a real misbehaviour and earns no
  entry in the quirk table, because every entry there is a *route around a defect* and this one needs
  none: the reader treats *set* and *reset* alike as available, so nothing is degraded. Two families
  do it.
- **A dotted underline has all four possible answers across four families**: spelled wrongly,
  unspelled, stored as a bit, and projected away before the reader can see it. That is why the
  suite has eight arms instead of one.
- **One capture surface distinguishes a cell the terminal emptied from a space somebody wrote** —
  and the reader throws that distinction away on purpose, with a test asserting both halves.
- **A terminal is not obliged to ignore a sequence it does not implement.** One prints it. That is
  why the alternate screen is entered by the first bytes of the capability negotiation and not
  afterwards: the echo lands on a page that is discarded.
